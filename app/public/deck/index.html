the problem, as a picture

One of these will hurt you.
Which one?

You cannot tell by looking. Neither can the pharmacist, and neither can the person paying for it. India’s drug regulator can — CDSCO names substandard and spurious medicines every single month.

■ flagged Not-of-Standard-Quality or spurious □ identical to the eye, the box and the strip
why nobody reads it

The warning exists.
It is just written in English, as a PDF.

CDSCO publishes the alert. It is a table of English brand names, batch numbers and manufacturers, behind a government portal, linked from a page most people will never open.

  • India’s 2011 Census records under 11% of the country speaking any English at all.
  • The list changes every month, so no static app and no model’s training data can hold it.
  • The person who needs it most is standing at a pharmacy counter right now, holding the strip.

A verdict, not a chatbot.

Ask by voice or by typing. Get back one card: red, green or amber — with the sources it read, in your language, read aloud.

sponsor api · 02

Anakin.io is where the facts come from.

3 credits

Search API

Live lookup for "<drug>" CDSCO NSQ alert. Returns this month’s alert pages — which are in no model’s training data, including the one answering.

1 credit

URL Scraper

The CDSCO alerts index and the WHO falsified-medicine index, scraped to markdown and handed to the model as regulator-of-record evidence.

2 credits

Wire · tmg_search

A typed action against Tata 1mg, India’s largest online pharmacy. Named parameters in, structured JSON out. No HTML ever reaches the model.

Why Wire and not a scraper we wrote ourselves

  • 1mg is a React storefront. A scraper against it is dead on their next deploy, and it dies silently, returning an empty page rather than an error.
  • A typed action has named parameters — query, city, per_page — so it survives a redesign we never hear about.
  • No raw HTML near the model means no prompt-injection surface from a page we do not control.

Our first catalog request is cdsco.gov.in itself — turn the regulator into a typed action and this stops being a scrape at all.

REMOVED
the kill test

Take Anakin out

No live regulator data, no WHO index, no price. What is left is a model guessing about drug safety from memory — which is the single most dangerous version of this product, and the reason we did not build it.

what the verdict is actually made of

Five authorities.
Zero opinions.

The model is not asked what it knows about a drug. It is handed evidence and asked to summarise it into a verdict, and it is rejected if it does not cite what it read.

the detail we are proudest of

Absence is evidence, not silence

A drug with no US label at all is not a missing field — it is a finding, and it is passed to the model as one. noUsLabel: true. Plenty of what circulates in India was never approved anywhere that publishes.

CDSCOIndia’s regulator. NSQ and spurious-drug alerts, refreshed monthly.
WHOThe global falsified & substandard medical product alert index — one scrape, reused across every drug in a prescription.
openFDA labelBoxed warnings, contraindications, drug interactions, approved use.
openFDA recallsEnforcement reports — product, reason, country, classification.
Tata 1mgWhat it costs in India today, so “is it safe” and “can I afford it” are one answer.

Four of the five are reached through Anakin. All five are read at query time, none is bundled, and every one of them can be clicked from the card.

the part that decides whether this is safe to ship

An answer we could not check
is never a green one.

Every evidence leg has its own failure lane. If CDSCO is unreachable, the verdict is forced to amber before it is rendered — and that response is never written to cache, so a bad minute cannot become a permanent wrong answer.

  • A failed leg pushes its name onto degraded[] and returns empty. It does not take the answer down with it.
  • The UI’s final state is read from that array, not from “the request finished”.
  • A verdict the model could not ground gets rewritten to “could not confirm”, which is the honest failure and the useless-looking one. We kept it anyway.

stated before anyone has to ask

What this is not

scope

Not medical advice

It is a regulatory-alert lookup and a second reader. The symptoms mode refuses to prescribe or dose, and says so out loud, in the user’s language, every single time.

data

Alert-level, not batch-level

CDSCO’s batch PDFs sit behind form.submit() POST forms. We cite the alerts index and live search hits — not individual batch numbers. Fixing that is a Wire catalog action, not a scraper.

model

The verdict is model-mediated

Which is why it is allowed to answer “could not confirm”, and does, rather than reaching for “safe” because that reads better in a demo.

coverage

openFDA is a US label

Used for pharmacology, not for Indian approval status. Where a drug has no US label we say so and treat it as a signal — we do not read it as clean.

Every one of these is in the submission text too. A demo that hides its edges is a demo that has not been driven near them.
